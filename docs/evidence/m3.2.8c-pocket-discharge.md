## What this shows

- **Step 2's working hypothesis is falsified.** Right-turners are not held through their green
  more often: about 0% before and after, with no residual queue at green's end. Their standing
  during green, discharge per green and headway barely move.
- **The whole rise is time standing at red:** +6.9 s South, +7.0 s North, which is step 2's
  +7.5 s. Nothing extra stands in front of a right-turner once its green comes.
- **"After" looks like random arrival; "before" did not.** A vehicle reaching the line at a
  uniformly random moment of a 120 s cycle waits on average R²/(2C) at red. That is 38.4 s for
  South (R = 96 s) and 40.8 s for North (R = 99 s), a rough bound, since standing excludes the
  approach. After M3.2.8b the pockets sit at 39.9 and 39.5 s. **Before, they sat about 7 s
  below it**: right-turners reached their line better timed to the green than random arrivals
  would. So the question is not what M3.2.8b added. It is what, before M3.2.8b, timed
  right-turners to their green, and why the lane-change model removed it.
- Step 2 found the pocket reached at the same mean time since departure, so the change is in
  *when in the cycle* right-turners arrive, not how long they travel.
