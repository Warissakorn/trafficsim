## Inputs

- `trafficsim-cli 42 --project data/projects/m2.6-study-template.traffic.json` and
  `... four-leg-signalised.traffic.json`, seed 42, one run each, unchanged project files.
- **Before:** the engine at `b472e05` (lane-fixed entry: a movement enters only on the lanes
  that reach it). **After:** this commit (every entry lane, mandatory changes, cooperation).

## The rule measured without cooperation, and rejected

The mandatory rule alone (contract §2 rules 1–5, no one holding back) gave, on M2.6 seed 42:
- mean delay **50.1 → 61.6 s**, **4 vehicles still pending** at the end;
- one vehicle waiting **404 s** at its dead end, blocking its lane behind it.

A dense target lane rarely leaves a gap both safety tests accept. The owner then ruled that
the smallest cooperation rule comes with this slice (contract §2 "Cooperation"), and the tables
above are with it.

## Not shown

- Performance (callgrind) and the desktop build. **Linux/GCC replay: done 2026-09-30** — GCC 15.2
  (WSL2, headless) reproduces every M2.6 figure above, before and after, digit for digit
  (`m3.2.8c-right-turns.md` §1). That evidence also shows seed 42 was not representative, and
  that the dead-end wait count above cannot be reproduced by the engine's own waiting test.
- Whether left-turn delay falls for the reason it seems to; no controlled case isolates it.
- The T-junction sweeps were not re-run: every Link in that fixture has one lane, so no route
  there has a stub and its runs cannot change (its `tjunction*` tests pass unchanged).
