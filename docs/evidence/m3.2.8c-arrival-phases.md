# M3.2.8c step 4: the right-turn rise was a five-seed sample, not a mechanism

Development evidence for `docs/NEXT.md`'s M3.2.8c step 4, after
[`m3.2.8c-pocket-discharge.md`](m3.2.8c-pocket-discharge.md). That step found the South → East
and North → West right-turn rise (+7.3, +7.5 s over seeds 42–46) to be time standing at red, and
that "before" right-turners reached their line about 7 s better timed than random arrival.
This step asked what timed them. **Not calibration and not M6 validation**; the
not-yet-validated marker stays. No behaviour changed in this session.

## Inputs

- `trafficsim-cli N --project data/projects/m2.6-study-template.traffic.json --arrival-phases
  --phase-bin 1`. The diagnostic is `ArrivalPhaseAccumulator` (`src/eval/arrival_phases.hpp`).
  Per movement and segment, it gives two histograms of the cycle phase (simulation time mod
  120 s). One is when the vehicle's front entered the segment; departure counts as entering the
  route's first segment, and a lane change never counts. The other is when it first stood there
  (below the queue counter's `beginSpeed`), counted only after it has once reached `endSpeed`.
  The first draft lacked that hysteresis and recorded every insertion as a stop on the entry
  Link, since vehicles enter at rest.
- **Before:** `b472e05`, with the diagnostic copied into a scratch worktree, uncommitted, behind
  an environment variable. **After:** this branch. In both, output with the diagnostic on equals
  output without it, the diagnostic's key apart (seeds 42–46, checked). **Linux only**: WSL2,
  GCC 15.2, headless Debug.
- Signals as in step 3. South green 0–24 s and North 58–79 s, then 3 s amber, so red is 27–120 s
  South and 82–178 s (mod 120) North. The "wait" below is the time from a phase to the next
  green start. Over a uniformly random phase it averages **36.0 s South and 38.4 s North**,
  with standard deviations of 30.6 and 31.4 s.

## 1. The timing is set at departure

For each right-turner, the wait implied by its phase on entering the pocket (`lane-31`,
`lane-43`), against the wait implied by its departure phase plus a constant 22 s. Step 2
measured 22 s as the mean time from departure to the pocket. Seeds 42–46 pooled, 1 s bins:

| | Pocket entry | Departure + 22 s | Uniform |
|---|---|---|---|
| South → East, before | 33.3 | 34.4 | 36.0 |
| South → East, after | 40.8 | 41.4 | 36.0 |
| North → West, before | 35.2 | 34.9 | 38.4 |
| North → West, after | 41.5 | 41.8 | 38.4 |

Pooled, the departure phase predicts the pocket wait within about 1 s in both engines. Per seed
it is within 2.5 s, except South, after, seed 43 (46.1 against 50.6). Right-turners cross the entry Link
without standing: after, 6 of 242 South right-turners stood there and no North one did; before,
none did. Departure delay is about 0.2 s (step 2), so the departure phase is the input's Poisson
draw. M3.2.8b adds an input per entry lane, which changes the order of draws from the one random
stream. A given seed therefore draws a different arrival sequence before and after.

## What this shows

- **Nothing timed the right-turners before M3.2.8b.** Over 40 seeds, both engines' pocket
  arrivals are uniform in the cycle. Seeds 42–46 happened to draw well-timed right-turn
  departures before and badly timed ones after. Step 3's "better than random" came from that
  sample, not from lane-fixed entry. South → East does not rise at all (−1.0 ± 1.1 s). North →
  West keeps +2.7 ± 1.2 s, of which the departure phase alone accounts for about 1.4 s
  (38.0 → 39.4).
- **So there is no ruling to make on the right turns.** The question NEXT reserved for the owner
  was whether the "before" timing was an artefact or behaviour to restore. It was neither: it
  was sampling.
- **M3.2.8b's real cost is the East approach.** East → West through rises by +6.0 ± 1.7 s and
  East → South left by +6.8 ± 2.6 s. The network mean rises by +2.4 ± 0.5 s, up in 34 of 40
  seeds. Step 1 put the long dead-end waits on the West and East left turns. That is where
  cooperation with a look-ahead (the next M3.2.8c row) is aimed. It is not shown here that the
  East rise *is* those waits.
- **The clamp increase is not systematic** (+1.4 ± 1.3, up in 20 of 40). The "doubled at seeds
  44 and 45" of step 1 is in the same five-seed sample.
- **Five seeds are not enough for a before/after comparison of one movement.** The per-seed
  standard deviation of a right-turn movement's delay change is about 7 s here. Steps 1–3 drew
  their movement-level conclusions from seeds 42–46. Their measurements stand. Their reading of
  the right turns as M3.2.8b's effect does not.

## Not shown

- Why the East approach rises: dead-end waits, cooperation holds, or the lane split itself.
  `--lane-changes` and `--segment-times` over 40 seeds would place it.
- The residual North → West +1.3 s beyond its departure phase.
- Windows/MSVC runs; the desktop build.
