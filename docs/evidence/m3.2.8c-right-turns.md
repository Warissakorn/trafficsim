# M3.2.8c step 1 — where lane changes happen, and the right-turn rise

Development evidence for `docs/NEXT.md`'s M3.2.8c step 1: *measure first, before changing
anything*. **Not calibration and not M6 validation**; the not-yet-validated marker stays. No
behaviour changed in this session: the default `trafficsim-cli --project` output is
byte-identical before and after it (six runs compared with `cmp`).

## Inputs

- `trafficsim-cli N --project data/projects/m2.6-study-template.traffic.json --lane-changes`,
  seeds 42–46, one run each, plus the four-leg fixture at seed 42.
- **Before M3.2.8b:** the engine at `b472e05`, with its own copy of the same template (the only
  difference in the file since then is `schemaVersion` 16 → 17). **After:** this commit.
- **Linux only**: WSL2 Ubuntu, GCC 15.2, headless Debug. No Windows run, no desktop build.
- The diagnostic (`LaneChangeAccumulator`, `src/eval/lane_changes.hpp`) books each vehicle on the
  movement it **arrives** on. *Before dead end* is the metres from the vehicle's front to its
  stub's dead end at the tick it changed. A dead-end wait is the engine's own test
  (`waitingAtDeadEnd`, the cooperation rule's "who is waiting"), counted in whole ticks.

## 1. Linux/GCC replays the Windows 8b evidence exactly

Seed 42, before and after, reproduces every figure in `m3.2.8b-mandatory.md` on GCC 15.2:
completed, active, clamps, mean delay, all twelve movements and all four queues. The 8b figures
were MSVC 19.51 on Windows. No toolchain difference showed in the printed digits.

## 4. Where the changes happen, and who waits (after, five seeds pooled)

| Movement | | Changes | Median before dead end (m) | Within 20 m of it | Waiting vehicles | Wait (s) | Longest (s) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| West → North | L | 293 | 272 | 9% | 19 | 347 | 78 |
| West → South | R | 216 | 215 | 5% | 2 | 2 | 1 |
| East → South | L | 262 | 272 | 7% | 16 | 518 | 98 |
| East → North | R | 213 | 215 | 7% | 6 | 89 | 50 |
| South → West | L | 151 | 272 | 3% | 4 | 8 | 2 |
| South → East | R | 127 | 215 | 2% | 1 | 3 | 3 |
| North → East | L | 129 | 272 | 2% | 2 | 135 | 81 |
| North → West | R | 100 | 215 | 0% | 0 | 0 | 0 |

Through movements make no change. 29 changes belong to vehicles still in the network at the end.

- **The median change happens at insertion.** 272 m and 215 m are each stub's whole span: the
  median vehicle changes in the tick it enters, and at least 90% of North → West's changes happen
  within 7 m of the network edge.
- **The robust right-turn rise has almost no dead-end waiting.** North → West: no waiting vehicle
  in five seeds. South → East: one vehicle, 3 s. Their changes are among the earliest of all.
- **Dead-end waits belong to the left turns on West and East** (35 vehicles, 865 s, up to 98 s),
  and those two movements' delay did not rise over five seeds (+0.7 and −2.3 s).
- The 8b evidence counted 14 vehicles and 406 s of dead-end waits at seed 42 by an ad-hoc count
  that was not recorded. The engine's own test gives 13 vehicles and 299 s. The figures here use
  the engine's test; the 8b figures cannot be reproduced.

The four-leg fixture, seed 42, agrees: medians at the start of the span, 2 waiting vehicles, 7 s.

## What this argues

- **Not `laneChangeDistance`.** Vissim's `laneChangeDistance` governs how far ahead of the
  divergence a driver starts the change. Here the right-turners already change at the first
  possible tick and hardly wait, so an earlier start cannot remove their added delay.
- **The South and North right-turn rise has another cause**, not found here. Candidates to
  measure next, per right-turn movement, before and after: departure delay at insertion (a
  stub vehicle now enters on the kerb lane and changes at once), time on the entry Link, and time
  from the pocket to the exit (the gap acceptance against the opposing stream, whose own lane use
  also changed).
- The long dead-end waits on the West and East left turns are what cooperation with a look-ahead
  (NEXT item 2) would address. They do not show in those movements' delay.
